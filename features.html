Features

What it does, in detail.

Three parts: the ordering page your buyers use, the shipping rules at your checkout, and the setup checklist that keeps you out of trouble.

01 · The ordering page

A wholesale order form on your own storefront.

It lives at your own web address, inside your own theme. Buyers sign in with their Shopify account and see only what their company can buy.

Two ways to order

Quick order is a dense list: product, MSRP, wholesale price, quantity, line total. Catalog is a picture grid for buyers who shop by eye. Buyers switch with one click, and the page remembers.

Their prices, from Shopify

Whatever catalog their company location is assigned. Fixed prices, percentage adjustments and volume price breaks all come through, because the page reads what Shopify already calculated.

Minimums and case sizes enforced

Type 5 where you sell in sixes and it becomes 6, with a note saying why. Maximums and stock limits are respected the same way, so buyers can't order what you can't ship.

Stock, shown honestly

Out of stock products can't be added. Low stock shows "Only 3 left" so a buyer can adjust rather than wait for an email from you.

Order the same as last time

A bar at the top offers their previous order. One click fills the form, quantities and all, adjusted to what's currently available.

Drafts that survive a closed tab

A part-typed order is saved in their browser. They come back tomorrow and it's still there, with a note saying when it was saved.

Search and categories

Search by product name or SKU. Optional category buttons across the top, plus an "ordered before" filter for repeat buyers.

Order notes and PO numbers

A note field that saves to the order as they type, so their PO number arrives with the order instead of in a separate email.

Looks like your store

Fonts and colours are inherited from your theme by default. Nothing about it says "third-party app", and you can override the accent colour if you prefer.

Works on a phone

Buyers place orders from the stockroom floor. The form reflows to one column, with quantity steppers big enough to use with a thumb.

One cart, one checkout

Adding from the order form uses your store's normal cart, so your theme's bag, slide-out cart and checkout all behave exactly as they already do. Buyers can add to their order from a product page too, if they want to.

02 · Shipping and checkout

Wholesale rates for wholesale buyers. Retail rates for everyone else.

Shopify shows every eligible shipping rate to every shopper. That's the single most common way a wholesale setup leaks money.

Rate tiers, built for you

Describe your tiers once — "$10 under $100, $20 to $250, free above $250" — and the app creates those rates in every shipping zone that needs them, in every profile.

Gating at the checkout itself

A Shopify delivery customization decides what each shopper sees. Wholesale buyers get wholesale rates; retail shoppers never see them.

It knows who's a wholesale buyer

From the company on the cart, not customer tags. Nobody has to remember to tag anyone, and a new contact at an existing company just works.

Local delivery and pickup

Offer them to everyone, to nobody, or only to company locations you approve — useful when you deliver to a few nearby accounts yourself.

It fails open, on purpose

If anything is misconfigured, buyers still get shipping options rather than an empty checkout. A lost sale is worse than a wrong-looking rate.

Clean removal

One button removes the rates it made. Uninstalling an app never cleans up after itself; this one tells you to do it first, and does it in a click.

03 · The setup checklist

It reads your store and tells you what's wrong.

Most wholesale problems aren't in the app, they're in the Shopify setup around it: a company with no contact, a location with no catalog, a catalog with no prices. The checklist finds those and links to the screen that fixes each one.

A real example of what it says

Needs fixing · 1 location can't see wholesale prices

Powderbound has no active catalog, so they pay your regular retail prices. Give it an active catalog.

Needs fixing · Wholesale shipping rules are off

Every shopper sees every shipping option at checkout, including your wholesale rates. Turn the rules on so retail shoppers only see retail rates.

Checks that matter

Companies without contacts, locations without catalogs, catalogs that raise prices or discount to zero, a theme that can't show the page, missing shipping rates.

It never guesses

If something couldn't be read, it says so rather than reporting a false all-clear. It will not tell you you're ready when you aren't.

Fix buttons, not instructions

Each item has a button that opens the exact Shopify screen, or performs the fix directly where the app is allowed to.
